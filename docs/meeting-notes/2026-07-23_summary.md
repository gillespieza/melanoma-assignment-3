Amanda presented her work on analyzing cancer datasets, focusing on four main pillars: pre-processing, clinical subtyping, genomic subtyping, and benchmarking of different models. She reported finding clear batch effects between TCGA and immunology datasets, identified key clinical features including tumor mutational burden and inflammation status, and developed response prediction models with XGBoost performing best for immunotherapy-treated trials. Amanda noted that some plots and tables need to be updated due to recent changes in her data processing approach, and she committed to updating the report structure before the end of the evening.

Amanda discussed the structure of their presentation, suggesting they focus on two key visuals: one showing which models perform best across different datasets and another illustrating why patients respond to immunotherapy based on immune signatures. She committed to providing suggested plots for the executive summary by Monday. Amanda also noted the extensive work involved in filtering relevant information from their extensive research.

### Survival Analysis Method Review

Hena reviewed Amanda's survival analysis work and requested clarification on control variables and statistical methods. Amanda explained her stratification approach, including definitions of "hot" and "cold" signatures, and confirmed she had performed univariate analysis but not multivariate analysis yet. Amanda agreed to add multivariate proportional hazard analysis to her list of next steps to better understand how multiple predictors interact together.

Amanda discussed her analysis of clinical features, noting that age was the most significant demographic factor across multiple datasets, while weight was only reported in TCGA. Hena suggested that BRAF mutations might not be effective predictors since patients are often pre-treated, and recommended considering multivariate plots while accounting for potential correlations between variables. Gift reported challenges finding specific drugs in the datasets and had to switch to a different drug, with plans to complete analysis by the next day, while Anthony provided an update on the ODE model implementation focusing on targeted therapy and immunotherapy branches.

### Tumor Burden Modeling Findings

Anthony presented findings on modeling tumor burden using an ODE model with three inputs: BRAF inhibitor, tumor burden, and MAPK signaling. The ODE model performed second best in terms of generalization ability compared to random forest, logistic regression, and neural network models. The next steps involve formulating final results and determining how this output will contribute to their decision tree narrative, with Anthony noting the need to make the results more interpretable and integrate them with other questions in their project.

She identified SOX10 as a promising novel target for melanoma treatment, with additional candidates including PSMD14 and others that may have repurposing potential from existing myeloma treatments.
